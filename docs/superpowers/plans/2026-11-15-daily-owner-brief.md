# The daily owner brief (F-082, focus-list row 14) — plan

Tier: HIGH (a new migration, a new table with grants, a customer-facing-style send to real inboxes on a schedule nobody watches, a signed link that writes). One implementer per PR, one review per PR with the mutation probes named below, a re-review only when a fix changes logic. Four PRs, each under about 800 changed lines, each shipping dormant until the last one turns it on.

Owner decisions (danlo, 2026-11-15, by tiles), binding: an EMAIL each morning at 7 AM in the account's own zone, in the person's language; today's bookings, yesterday's calls and leads Sofía handled, and open To-dos; OPT-IN, off until the person turns it on in their own Mis preferencias; SKIP quiet days; texting comes later (out of scope, a seam is left).

## 1. What it is, in one paragraph

Each morning, for each person who opted in on a business they belong to, one plain email from that business's brand: what is on the calendar today, what Sofía handled yesterday, what is waiting for them. One email per (person, business), never merged across businesses. No AI writes any of it: every sentence is deterministic copy filled from the same reads the owner line already speaks from, so the email and the phone call can never disagree about "your day" (F-032 §5; `lib/owner-line/tools/read.ts`). F-082 is catalogued as "folded into F-100" (`docs/crm-features.md:2130`); F-100's metrics catalogue is not built, so this ships on the existing reads and keeps all shaping in ONE module (`brief-content.ts`) that F-100 can later replace.

## 2. Why a quiet day is skipped when a quiet week is not

DESIGN.md "The weekly report" says a quiet week still sends, "because silence leaves a client unable to tell a quiet week from a broken product". That reasoning is about cadence. A weekly mail is the product's proof of life: one a week, so missing it is noticed, and a "nothing happened" week is itself the answer to "is it working?". A daily mail with nothing in it is noise seven days in a row, trains the owner to stop opening it, and then the one morning it matters goes unread. The proof-of-life job is already done by the weekly report, which still sends on quiet weeks (unchanged). The brief is a convenience the person chose; it speaks only when it has something to say. To keep "skipped" from looking like "broken", the person's own Preferences row says when the last one was sent. `docs/crm-features.md:2870` (row 13, "Proposed, needs the owner's dated decision") says the quiet-week rule "also binds the morning brief": today's decision supersedes that line, and the plan proposes the matching DESIGN.md and catalogue edits (section 9). The other two weekly rules do bind the brief: deltas in words never arrows, and a metric not measured is omitted, never zeroed.

## 3. Decisions (each with its reason)

**D1. Who receives.** A person who (a) has a row in the new subscription table for this business, AND (b) is still, at send time, a member of that business with role `owner` or `staff`, AND (c) the business is not `outbound_suppressed`, not archived, and has `client_access_enabled`. Technicians never receive it. Reason: the content is read with the service role scoped by account id (the owner line's F2 path, `data-path.ts`), which equals what an Owner or Staff member sees; a Technician's scope lives only in RLS (0068) and a service read would show them every customer. The role list is its own constant, `DAILY_BRIEF_ROLES`, NOT an alias of `OWNER_LINE_ROLES`: if the owner line later flips to the token path and widens to technicians, the brief must not widen with it. A test pins that technician is absent. Membership is re-read on every send, so a person removed from the team stops receiving the same day. Whether Staff should be offered it at all is OWNER QUESTION Q1.

**D2. Where the opt-in lives.** A NEW server-only table `owner_brief_subscriptions`, not a column on `memberships` or `users`. Row present = on; turning off deletes the row. Reasons: a person in two businesses chooses per business (one row each); `memberships` carries team-management writes and a stamp column there could be forged or clobbered by them; the idempotency stamp belongs next to the opt-in it guards. The shape follows 0115's `owner_line_enrollments`: RLS on with NO policy, every grant revoked from `anon`, `authenticated` and `service_role` by name, then `select, insert, update, delete` to `service_role` only. Written only by server actions keyed on Clerk's `userId` from `auth()` (never a person id from the browser), exactly the `preferences/actions.ts` and `owner-line-actions.ts` pattern, including its refusal of an impersonation session (`actor`).

```
owner_brief_subscriptions
  id               uuid pk default gen_random_uuid()
  user_id          uuid not null references users(id) on delete cascade
  account_id       uuid not null references accounts(id) on delete cascade
  enabled_at       timestamptz not null default now()
  last_handled_day date                       -- the account-local day last decided (sent OR quiet)
  last_sent_day    date                       -- the account-local day last actually sent
  last_outcome     text check (last_outcome in ('sent','quiet'))
  created_at       timestamptz not null default now()
  unique (user_id, account_id)
```
The migration is created with `pnpm --filter @bis/db migration:new owner_brief_subscriptions` (timestamped name; `0001`..`0116` keep their numbers). Header comment states apply order (bis-ci via CI, production via the click), rollback (`drop table`), and the grants. No SQL functions: the claim in D4 is one conditional UPDATE.

**D3. The Preferences UI.** One new card, "Your daily brief" ("Su resumen diario"), under the two preference cards and above or beside the owner-line cards, one ROW per qualifying business (a membership in `DAILY_BRIEF_ROLES`, `client_access_enabled`, not archived; the same `listMyMemberships` read the owner-line card uses, `owner-line/memberships.ts`). Each row is an immediate switch with an Undo toast, DESIGN rule 6 (reversible, so it runs at once), which is also how the language and theme cards already behave. A switch is not a Save, so rule 8 is untouched: the card carries no primary. Under an ON row: "Arrives at 7 AM, your time. First one tomorrow." and "Last one: Tuesday." (from `last_sent_day`, absent until there is one). Own loading skeleton, empty state ("You are not on a team that gets a brief"), and error state per card, per rule 5. The card is hidden for an impersonation session. Copy goes under a new `dailyBrief.*` namespace in `messages.ts`, EN and ES (usted, "su negocio"), through `t()`; the 7 AM read applies in both languages.

**D4. Timing and idempotency.** A pass `dailyOwnerBrief`, one line in `registry.ts` and one node in `fanout.ts`'s `PLAN` (`after: ["billingPause"]`, `settled`, like the weekly reports), which runs every 15 minutes with everything else. No second scheduler. Per subscription, in this order, each refusal counted under its own name: account zone unresolvable (`resolveAccountZone`) → account-local hour not in the band 07:00 to 08:59 (`dailyBriefBand(now, zone)`, same machinery as `inMondayBand`) → the person enabled it today or later (`enabled_at`'s local date is not before today's: it starts the next morning, and a toggle off-on can never double-send) → already handled for today's local date → tick cap → tick budget → compute → quiet? → send → stamp.
 - **Local days, never UTC.** "Today" and "yesterday" are `localDayStart(day, zone)` from `owner-line/range.ts` (the first instant whose local date is the day; correct on 23 and 25 hour days) via `resolveRange({range:"today"|"yesterday"}, zone, now)`. Data days are UTC and owners are US Central: nothing here ever slices `created_at` by UTC date.
 - **DST.** The band keys on wall-clock hour in the zone, so the clock change (2 AM) never touches 7 AM; the day windows are the 23 and 25 hour days `localDayStart` already handles. Tests run Chicago, New York, Denver, Los Angeles, Phoenix (no DST) and Honolulu, on a normal day and on both 2027 change days.
 - **Claim, then send, then release on failure.** The pass first CLAIMS the day with one atomic conditional UPDATE (`set last_handled_day = <today> where id = <id> and (last_handled_day is null or last_handled_day < <today>) returning id`); zero rows means another tick or a replayed dispatch has it, counted `skippedAlreadyHandled`, never sent. Only the claimant sends. A definite send failure RELEASES the claim (sets `last_handled_day` back to its previous value) so the next tick inside the band retries, per the repo's "a failed send is retried by an unstamped row" rule. This is deliberately NOT the weekly report's send-then-stamp: that order tolerates a duplicate when the stamp write fails, and the dispatcher allows a replayed tick (`parseTickNow` accepts a `now` up to 900 s old), so two runs can overlap. Trade made on purpose: a function killed between claim and send loses that day's brief (at most once); a duplicate morning email is judged worse than a missed one, and the next day is unaffected. A missed band is a missed day: a 10 AM "here is your 7 AM day" is stale and is never sent.
 - **Caps (`caps.ts`)**: `DAILY_BRIEF_TICK_CAP` and `DAILY_BRIEF_BUDGET_MS`, with the pass-route fit check (`F-196`, one send per subscription), so a zone full of subscribers cannot outrun the 90 s route. Capped subscriptions are not lost: the claim is the cursor, and the band is eight ticks wide.

**D5. Content rules** (`brief-content.ts`, pure shaping over the reads; `brief-gate.ts`, pure and tested alone).
 - *Today's bookings*: `appointments(ctx, {range:"today"})` (listAgendaBookings), status `booked` only, so a cancelled job or a replaced one never shows; time, customer name, meeting type, in the person's locale and the account's zone. Never the booking note (owner-line SC-10).
 - *Yesterday's calls handled*: `callsAndMessages(ctx, {range:"yesterday"})`, which counts what the dashboard's "Calls answered" tile counts: outcomes `booked`, `lead`, `message`, `transferred`, excluding the agency's own handsets and owner-line calls. Spam is therefore excluded by construction (outcome `spam`, the rule #349 and `weekly-metrics.ts`'s `ANSWERED_OUTCOMES` use); a robocall, a hang-up (`abandoned`) and a timeout record never count and are not mentioned.
 - *Yesterday's leads*: `listLeadInstantsBetween` (form submissions plus calls with outcome `lead`), the same read the Monday report uses, so a lead is one number everywhere. A lead taken by call appears in both the calls line and the leads line, and the copy says so once rather than adding them.
 - *Open To-dos*: `needsAttention` (listAccountWork): the real total, the first five by the queue's own order, each a `workRowLabel` line, then "and N more" (never a silent cap).
 - *Quiet day, exactly* (`isQuietDay`): no booking today with status `booked`, AND no answered call yesterday, AND no lead yesterday, AND no To-do that is due today or overdue (`dueAt` before tomorrow's start) or new since yesterday began (`occurredAt` at or after yesterday's start). An old, undated backlog never makes a day non-quiet on its own; it rides along (count and top five) whenever something else triggers a send. Otherwise a 40-item backlog would mail the owner every morning forever.
 - *Deltas*: none. A daily brief states the day, not a comparison; nothing is zeroed or arrow-ed. A section with nothing in it is omitted ("no calls yesterday" is not printed; the subject carries what exists).
 - *Words*: subject "Your day at {business}: 3 jobs today, 2 calls yesterday" (parts present only, plural-correct in both languages), `{business}` the brand name (`brandDisplayName`), never `accounts.name`. Body from the shell (`emailBrandNamed`), text and html both composed by hand, links to `/dashboard/accounts/<id>/calendar`, `/calls` and `/tasks`, origin from `ctx.origin` (`APP_ORIGIN`). No `{{template_syntax}}`, no milestone codes (an `INTERNAL_MILESTONE` guard test, as both copy catalogues carry), no carrier or vendor words. One message, one language: subject, body, dates (Intl locale `es-US`/`en-US`), button, footer.
 - *Isolation*: every read takes the one `accountId` of the subscription and nothing else; a person in two businesses gets two emails, each from its own brand. Nothing from business B can enter business A's message because B's data is never read in that iteration.
 - *Language*: `resolveLocale(users.language, accounts.language)`, then English: the person's own choice first, because this email is to the person, not a customer.

**D6. Failure and observability.** Per subscription, a try/catch; one bad address or one account's failing read never stops the next (the weekly pass's isolation). Counters stay honest: `sent` only after the provider returned; `failed` on a thrown send or read; `skippedQuiet`, `skippedAlreadyHandled`, `skippedNotInBand`, `skippedNotYet`, `skippedCap`, `skippedBudget`, `unresolvableTimezone`, `released`. The harness writes the `cron.pass.dailyOwnerBrief` heartbeat every tick (a pass that throws is `{ errored: 1 }`, tick continues), so the agency sees it ran and `opsWatch` alerts after two consecutive failures. Quiet-day skips are counted, so "all quiet" and "broken" are distinguishable in the tick JSON. The email kind `operator.daily_brief` (`classes.ts`: class operator, hours any, footer none, like `operator.weekly_report`) goes through the one email gate, which already enforces `outbound_suppressed` and names the kind at exactly one site (`scans.test.ts`). The pass writes no `automation_log` row: that table's `source` is a CHECK-constrained list (0046/0047) read by the client's history, and a brief to a team member is not a customer message; skipping it avoids a second migration.

**D7. Unsubscribe.** Every brief carries a plain "Turn this off" link in its body, and (if the consent owner agrees, see section 8) the RFC 8058 `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers. The token is a NEW signed token (`lib/brief/token.ts`): payload `{ v:1, s:<subscription id>, i:<issued ms> }`, HMAC-SHA256, constant-time compare, MAC checked first. Keys derive from the existing `CONSENT_TOKEN_SECRET` through HKDF with its OWN info label (`bis-brief-token`), so no new env var and a consent token can never open a brief token or the reverse (a test pins both ways). Not encrypted: it holds one uuid and no address. It never expires: it only ever turns a preference OFF, which is idempotent and the safe direction, and a link in an old email should still work. Routes mirror the customer pair exactly: `POST /api/brief/off/[token]` is the one-click (deletes the row, 200 even if already gone), `GET` redirects to a public page `/brief/off/[token]` whose button posts the same action, so a mail scanner that prefetches the link cannot silently turn anyone's brief off. The page says it is off and links to Preferences to turn it back on. Public like `/u` and `/api/unsubscribe` (the `proxy.ts` allow-list is the implementer's first check).

## 7. Risks

 - A function killed between claim and send loses that day's brief (D4's stated trade). Visible as `sent` lower than the opted-in count with no `failed`; acceptable at one email a day.
 - `users.email` is the address captured from Clerk at a person's first save. If it can go stale after a Clerk email change, the brief goes to the old address. UNVERIFIED; PR 1 step 0 finds out, and the fix (read the live Clerk address at send, or refresh on change) is its own decision.
 - The service-role reads are the owner line's F2 path: correct only because recipients are Owner and Staff. Mitigated by `DAILY_BRIEF_ROLES` being separate and a pinned test; if the data path ever moves to `token`, revisit.
 - Gmail discards mail sent from a `vercel.app` origin (cron facts); links use `APP_ORIGIN`. With it unset the pass must refuse to send rather than mail a dead link: PR 3 adds that refusal, tested.
 - A bounced address fails the same way every morning; counted `failed`, retried within the band, and it will trip `opsWatch` after two consecutive failing ticks only if the pass itself throws, not per address. Not solved here; noted.
 - F-100 may later change what the brief says. All shaping is in `brief-content.ts` behind a typed seam, so that change touches one module.

## 8. OWNER QUESTIONS (each with a recommendation)

 - **Q1. Owner only, or Staff too?** Recommend Owner and Staff, each by their own opt-in. Staff already see the same calendar, calls and To do on the dashboard and on the owner line, nothing is sent unless the person chooses it, and a shop's front-desk person is often who needs the morning list. If you want it Owner-only, `DAILY_BRIEF_ROLES = ["owner"]` is the one-line change.
 - **Q2. Is a To-do alone enough to send on?** Recommend: only a To-do due today or overdue, or one that arrived since yesterday, triggers a send; an old undated backlog rides along but never triggers. The alternative (any open To-do sends) means a client with a stale backlog is emailed every day.
 - **Q3. What if the brief is late?** Recommend: send only from 7:00 to 8:59 local, then drop that day. A 10 AM "your day" is stale; the next morning is unaffected. The alternative (send whenever it recovers, until noon) risks a duplicate-feeling message mid-morning.
 - **Q4. Does enabling it send one right away?** Recommend no: first one the next morning. Predictable, and it makes off-on in one day unable to double-send. A "send me one now" button is a separate feature and would bypass the day stamp.
 - **Q5. `List-Unsubscribe` headers on this operator mail.** Needs the consent owner's yes: the gate sets those headers only for customer kinds and `EmailRequest` omits `headers` on purpose. Recommend yes, for this one kind only (a signed link the pass supplies), because Gmail and Outlook show their own "unsubscribe" button from it. Without it the body link still ships.
 - **Q6. The documents that say otherwise.** `docs/crm-features.md` row 13 ("also binds the morning brief") is overridden by today's decision; recommend the edits in section 9 so the next planner is not misled.
