# After the job: a two-question check-in and reviews without gating (F-072, focus-list row 11) — plan

Tier: HIGH (a new migration with a table, grants and a `tasks` trigger change; a customer-facing send on a schedule nobody watches; a public signed link that writes; consent and quiet hours). One implementer per PR, one review per PR with the mutation probes named below, a re-review only when a fix changes logic. Four PRs, each under about 800 changed lines, each shipping dormant until the last turns it on.

Owner decisions (danlo, 2026-11-17, by tiles), binding: EMAIL first, in the customer's language; TEXT later per account once its texting registration clears (a seam, out of scope). The ask is a one-tap "How did we do?" (great, okay, not great), and THEN the review link is shown to EVERYONE whatever they answered; a "not great" also alerts the owner. Sent 2 hours after the booking is marked completed, inside the sending hours; at most ONE request per customer per 90 days; never after an opt-out. The owner or the agency pastes the Google review link once; no Google API.

## 1. What exists today (read from source) and what F-072 does to it

F-072 is a CHANGE to the existing `review_request` recipe, not a second system. Today, from source:
 - **Trigger.** A person's "Mark completed" (`setBookingStatus`, `packages/db/src/booking.ts:452`) sets `bookings.completed_at`. `listDueReviewRequests` (`packages/db/src/automations.ts:299`) takes completed bookings with `review_requested_at is null`, the recipe ON, a live contact, inside `REVIEW_REQUEST_MAX_AGE_MS` (61 h).
 - **Clock and gate.** The clock runs from `laterOf(endsAt, completedAt)` (`anchor.ts`). `shouldSendReviewRequestNow` (`review-request-gate.ts`) needs the 08:00 to 11:00 morning band on a strictly LATER local day than the anchor, and defers to the calendar follow-up email (rule 4: the follow-up is day one, the review day two).
 - **Channel and copy.** `config = { channel: "email" | "sms", reviewUrl }` (`parseReviewRequestConfig`, https and http only, 2048 chars, normalised on write too). The body is operator-written (or the default "Thanks for choosing {name}! ... a quick review:") in the contact's language (`chooseBody`), with the review link as the email's button or the text's tail. There is NO check-in: the link is the whole ask.
 - **Consent.** Email kind `automation.review_request` is class `marketing`, hours `automated` (08:00 to 21:00 daily), footer `unsubscribe`, and is in `FOOTER_ADDRESS_KINDS` (`lib/consent/classes.ts:108,189`); the one email gate reads the ledger, `holdOrSend` HOLDS a send outside the hours and `release-held` brings it back. `loadSendableRows` and the shared `outbound-suppressed` test cover suppression.
 - **Stamps and limits.** `bookings.review_requested_at` (send-then-stamp, `stampWithRetry`), `review_request_sms_failed_at`; the recipe caps (`caps.ts`: tick cap, daily cap per account). The dedupe is PER BOOKING only: a customer with three completed jobs can be asked three times.
 - **Neighbours that read it.** The referral ask waits for it (`reviewRequestStillOwed`, `referral-ask-gate.ts`) and then fires the next morning; the follow-up pass runs first in `registry.ts`; the settings card (`automations/automations-settings.tsx`, `saveReviewRequestAction`) is AGENCY-ONLY and English-only (`actions.ts:66`, the layout's `EnglishOnlyLayout`).

**Kept:** the recipe key, the pass, the due-list and its by-id twin, the `review_requested_at` stamp as the one idempotency stamp, the email kind and its class (an opt-out, the hours, the footer and the address all keep working unchanged), `holdOrSend`, the caps, `config.reviewUrl` and its validation, the SMS channel exactly as it is (a direct-link text; the check-in needs a page the text can carry, and texting waits on registration), `anchor.ts`.
**Changed:** a new `checkin` flag in the jsonb config (absent means false, so every account behaves exactly as today until its flag is set; no data migration). With `checkin: true` on the EMAIL channel the pass sends the check-in email 2 hours after the anchor (not the next morning), adds the 90-day-per-customer rule (to both channels), and ignores any operator-typed body. The referral ask and the follow-up each gain one guard (D6, D7).
**Added:** a table `job_checkins` (answers), a public branded page `/r/<token>`, one To-do per "not great", a record-timeline row, a counts line and the switch on the settings card.
**Removed:** nothing. The "wait one more morning for the follow-up" rule stays for the non-check-in path.

## 2. Decisions (each with its reason)

**D1. The landing page `/r/<token>`.** A new public tree like `/u` and `/d` (own `layout.tsx` with its own `<html>`, `publicFormTheme` and `PublicBrand`, so the client's logo and brand colour carry, DESIGN rule 9; `robots: noindex`, `referrer: no-referrer`). `proxy.ts`'s matcher lists the public trees (`"/((?!_next|c/|f/|b/|u/|d/|s/|...)"`); `r/` is added there and in `proxy.test.ts` (bis-platform's file: needs in section 9). The page speaks the language the EMAIL was sent in: the language is inside the signed token, so it cannot be tampered with and the email, page and review button are one language end to end.
 - **A GET only reads; a press writes.** This is the repo's rule for `/u` (a mail scanner that prefetches a link records nothing). So the email carries ONE button ("Tell us how we did") that opens the page, and the three answers are three big buttons on the page, each a server action. To the customer it is one question and one tap on the answer. Three answer links inside the email would let a link scanner record "great", "okay" and "not great" for a person who never opened it, and would raise a false owner alert. See OWNER QUESTION Q1.
 - **First answer wins, idempotently.** The action inserts `job_checkins` with `on conflict (booking_id) do nothing returning`; a second tap, a double submit or a replay records nothing, creates no second To-do, and shows the same thank-you with the answer they gave.
 - **Then the link, to everyone.** After any answer the page shows the same thank-you block and the SAME review button (the account's `reviewUrl`, read at render time, never carried in the token). The only per-answer difference is one sentence above it: great and okay "Thank you. If you have a minute, a review helps {name} a lot."; not great "We're sorry we missed the mark. We've let {name} know. If you'd still like to leave a review, here is the link." The button, its href and its prominence are identical for all three answers, built by ONE component with no answer parameter in its props, so a code path that hides it cannot exist unnoticed (probe P4). No copy before the answer mentions the review, so the check-in is never a filter in front of it. Spanish: usted, "su negocio"; the words are written in PR 2 and read at 7 AM in both languages.
 - **The link opening is counted without a tracker.** The review button is a `<form>` whose action stamps `review_link_opened_at` (first press only) and 303-redirects to the URL. It is a POST, so a scanner cannot trigger it; it works with no JavaScript; the owner sees "N opened the link", never who left a review (we cannot know and do not pretend to). No pixels: the repo's email shell loads no tracking image (only the brand logo), and this plan adds none.
 - **Dead ends.** A token that fails its MAC is an UNBRANDED "this link is not valid" page, both languages stacked, with no reads (no account or language can be trusted before the MAC verifies). A token past 30 days still shows the review button (a late reviewer is still welcome) and records nothing and alerts nobody. A booking since deleted or moved to another account is "not valid", never another business's data (D2's re-read).
 - **The token.** `lib/checkin/token.ts`: payload `{ v:1, a:<account id>, b:<booking id>, l:"en"|"es", i:<issued ms> }`, HMAC-SHA256 with a constant-time compare, MAC checked before any parse of the payload; keys derived from the existing `CONSENT_TOKEN_SECRET` through HKDF with its OWN domain label (`bis-checkin-token`, the HKDF SALT with info `mac-v1`, matching the consent and brief tokens; shared code in `lib/signed-token.ts`), so no new env var and a consent or brief token can never open a check-in token or the reverse (pinned both ways, as the brief plan pins its own). Not encrypted: two uuids, no address. If the daily-brief PR 2 has landed its token helper, extract the shared HKDF and MAC code rather than copy it (the implementer's first read). Unset secret: the page answers "temporarily unavailable" with zero queries, and the pass refuses to send a link it cannot sign.

**D2. Where the answer lives, and who sees it.** A new table `job_checkins` (`id`, `account_id`, `booking_id`, `contact_id`, `answer` check in ('great','okay','not_great'), `answered_at`, `review_link_opened_at`, `created_at`; `unique (booking_id)`; AS SHIPPED in PR 1 (migration `20261117131959_job_checkins`): ONE key `(account_id, booking_id, contact_id)` onto a new `bookings` unique key `(account_id, id, contact_id)`, ON UPDATE CASCADE ON DELETE CASCADE, and no key straight into `contacts`. So a row can point at no other account's booking and at no customer but its booking's own, and it follows a contact merge and its Undo through `bookings.contact_id` with no merge function re-created; a direct key into `contacts` would have tripped the merge and held-by guards that pin every key into contacts). A row exists only once the customer answered; "sent" is `bookings.review_requested_at`, so there is one stamp and no second source of truth. The action NEVER trusts the token for the contact: it re-reads the booking by `(account, booking)` with the service client, requires `status = completed`, and takes `contact_id` from that row.
 - Grants: RLS on; members of the account READ their own rows (the account-member select policy shape of the most recent member-readable table, the implementer copies it and names which in the migration header); every write is `service_role` only, and the public action runs on the service client scoped by the token's account. `anon` and `authenticated` get no write, pinned by a grants test run before and after.
 - On the record: a new `TimelineEntry` kind `"checkin"` beside `documentShareOpened` (`packages/db/src/record.ts:372,674`), author mark **the customer** (`author-mark.ts`'s closed set of five, no sixth kind), text "Answered the check-in: Great" / "Not great" with the booking's day; a "not great" row also carries the To-do's state. Rule 3 applies: the answer is dot plus word, never colour alone.
 - Not an event on `events`: that table is for domain events (`emit`), not a customer-visible fact the record must render.

**D3. What "not great" triggers.** Exactly ONE To-do, and no auto-reply. `tasks` gets a nullable `review_checkin_id` (FK to `job_checkins(id)`, `on delete cascade`) and a partial unique index `tasks_review_checkin_once`, the exact 0084 pattern (`ensureDocumentExpiryTask`, `packages/db/src/document-expiry.ts:62`: insert, treat 23505 as "already there"). The migration RE-EMITS `app.keep_task_links` in full, because 0097 (not 0084) holds its latest definition and `create or replace` of an older copy would silently drop later columns: the implementer reads the CURRENT body from `0097_one_switch.sql` first and adds the one column to both branches. `author-mark.ts` gets the one-line case (a To-do with `review_checkin_id` is the automation's mark, as `document_expiry_id` is). Title: "Call {customer}: they said the job was not great" (Spanish twin), contact and booking linked, due now, so it lands in Today on the To do screen and in the opt-in daily brief. The To-do comes from the same action, after the insert that won the conflict, so a replay cannot make a second. The owner is ALSO emailed at once (owner decision 2026-11-17, Q4): the booking calendar's `notify_emails` get an `operator.checkin_alert` email, ONLY when the call that made the To-do reports `created: true` (the answer page and the sweep alike), so it goes at most once per check-in. The To-do is the record; the email is the nudge. It lives in `lib/checkin/alert.ts`, never throws, and a failure or an empty list leaves the To-do untouched (counted, not retried).

**D4. Eligibility and limits.** All must hold, each refusal counted under its own name:
 - Booking `status = completed` only (the due-list already filters it; no-show and cancelled never qualify, and a booking marked completed then reverted before the send drops out at the by-id re-read, `getDueReviewRequestById`).
 - The contact has a deliverable email (channel email; a missing one is `skippedNoAddress`), is not archived (the due-list), and the address is not opted out (the email gate reads the ledger for `marketing`), and the account is not `outbound_suppressed` (`loadSendableRows`). No new consent logic is written; the check-in inherits the chain by keeping the kind.
 - The recipe is ON and `config` parses with a review link; with `checkin: true` the account also has a mailing address (the marketing footer prints it, `marketing-footer.ts`; no address, no send, counted, as the referral ask does).
 - **Timing.** `shouldSendCheckinNow(now, anchor, zone)`, pure and tested alone: zone resolves (else fail closed), `now - anchor >= 2 h`, and `<= CHECKIN_MAX_AGE_MS` = 36 h (derived in the file's comment as 2 h delay + 11 h longest hold (21:00 to 08:00) + 1 h fall-back night = 14 h on schedule, and 22 h of slack for the caps and a failed tick; pinned in the test with a real zone and a DST change, as the 61 h figure is). NOT the morning band and NOT "strictly later local day". The sending hours come from the consent chain, not the gate: `holdOrSend` already holds an `automated` email outside 08:00 to 21:00 in the account's zone and `release-held` sends it at the opening, so a job completed at 19:30 is due at 21:30, outside the window, and waits for 08:00 the next day, still inside the 36 h. The anchor stays `laterOf` (0026), so a week of jobs marked completed on Friday afternoon is one burst two hours later, bounded by the caps.
 - **90 days per customer.** New `recentReviewAsks(db, accountId, { contactId, email }, sinceIso)` in `packages/db/src/automations.ts`: true when ANY other booking in the same account has `review_requested_at` after `now - 90 d` and belongs to the same `contact_id` OR to a contact with the same lower-cased email. Keyed on the ADDRESS as well as the contact because the address is what receives mail (and what the consent ledger keys on): two contact records for one person, or a household sharing one address, are one recipient. Across accounts it does NOT apply (the customer of two businesses is asked by each, and the accounts are separate tenants). The pass also remembers addresses sent earlier in the SAME tick (two jobs completed together must send one email, not two, which the DB read alone cannot see until the first stamp lands). Stamps made by the old flow count, so there is no cliff on deploy. A refused row stays unstamped until it ages out of the 36 h window and is counted `skippedRecentlyAsked`. Applies to the SMS channel by contact id.
 - **Idempotency.** Unchanged, and the one thing never loosened: send, then `stampWithRetry(stampReviewRequested)`; `unstamped` is counted when the stamp fails. The check-in send also carries the provider key `review-checkin/<booking>`, because its due span is ~34 h (the provider deduplicates a retry for 24 h). The answer table adds a second, independent guard (`unique (booking_id)`) for the page.
 - **Caps and the burst.** The existing recipe caps stand. A check-in sends in the afternoon, so many accounts' ticks are live all day, not one band: the implementer confirms the tick cap's route-fit arithmetic (`caps.ts`) still holds with an all-day window.

**D5. Settings.** The review link and the switch stay in the existing Review requests card (agency-only, English, `saveReviewRequestAction`; one primary Save per card, DESIGN rule 8, unchanged). Added: a switch "Ask how it went first, then offer the review link" (saved as `config.checkin`, email channel only; selecting Text hides it, because a text cannot carry the check-in until its account is registered), and an "Open link" anchor beside the URL field (`target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"`, no server call) so the agency tests what customers will land on. The https requirement: stored links stay http or https as today (existing accounts may hold http), but the check-in switch refuses an `http:` link and says so; the validator is one function (`parseReviewRequestConfig`) used on read and write, extended and tested there. The message box is hidden while the switch is on: the check-in's words are ours, fixed and bilingual, because an agency-typed paragraph ("if you loved us, leave a review") is exactly the gating this feature exists to remove. The owner (client) cannot edit it: decision 29 keeps marketing recipes agency-set. OWNER QUESTION Q3.

**D6. The email.** `reviewCheckinEmail(...)` beside `review-request.ts`, through the shared shell and `proseWithButton` (logo, brand colour, the unsubscribe footer, the RFC 8058 headers and the mailing address come from the shell and the gate, not from the template). Same kind `automation.review_request`, so `classes.test.ts` and `scans.test.ts` need no new row. English: subject "{name}: how did we do?" (owner copy decision: the earlier "How did we do, {name}?" read as addressing the business), body "Thanks for choosing {name}. One quick question: how did we do?", button "Tell us how we did". Spanish: "¿Cómo le fue con {name}?", "Gracias por elegir a {name}. Una pregunta rápida: ¿cómo le fue?", "Cuéntenos cómo nos fue". `{name}` is `brandDisplayName` (the due-row's `brandName`), never `accounts.name`; the subject and body say nothing about reviews. Both the text and html parts carry the same single link. No template syntax, no milestone codes (an `INTERNAL_MILESTONE` guard test, as both copy catalogues have), no vendor words. The link is built on `APP_ORIGIN` only; the pass refuses when APP_ORIGIN is not configured (`ctx.origin` falls back to the cron's `vercel.app` host, which Gmail discards), counted `skippedNoLink`, a tested refusal.

**D7. The neighbours.** (a) **Follow-up.** The calendar follow-up ("How did it go?") fires the morning after; with the check-in the same afternoon that would be a second "how did it go". The follow-up due-list skips a booking whose `review_requested_at` is set AND whose account has `checkin: true`; if the check-in did not send, the follow-up still goes. OWNER QUESTION Q2. In check-in mode `shouldSendCheckinNow` does not defer to `followup_sent_at` (a follow-up that went on an earlier day is a different ask). (b) **Referral ask.** It would otherwise fire the next morning, asking a customer who said "not great" whether they know anyone. The referral due-list excludes any booking with a `not_great` check-in, and nothing else changes: only the `not_great` exclusion (a tested guard); re-timing the ladder is not part of F-072.

**D8. Measurement, minimal.** One read, `checkinCounts(db, accountId, sinceIso)`: asked (`review_requested_at`, check-in accounts), answered, great, okay, not great, opened the review link; last 30 days, on the settings card (agency-visible). "Opened" counts only presses that have a `job_checkins` row: a press from the expired, unanswered view is not counted. The client owner sees each answer on the customer's record and each "not great" in To do. NOT in the weekly report in this plan: its contract is four measured numbers, F-100's catalogue is not built, and a count that is zero because the feature is off would break the omit-never-zero rule; when added it is omitted for accounts without `checkin`. OWNER QUESTION Q6.

## 5. Acceptance (danlo, on BIS's own account; no script sends to a real address)

## 6. Risks

 - **Review-gating law and Google's policy** are the owner's stated reason for D1; they are taken as given and NOT independently verified here (counsel confirms before the first paying client). The design keeps to them structurally: no pre-answer review wording, one component, identical link.
 - **A "not great" To-do is a promise to act.** The page says "We've let {name} know" and that is true only because the To-do exists; an owner who never opens To do breaks the promise. Q4's email is the mitigation: it goes AT MOST ONCE per check-in (only when the To-do was created), and a failed `task.created` event after the insert no longer loses it (`ensureCheckinTask` logs that failure and still answers `created: true`). A calendar with no `notify_emails`, or a send that fails, leaves the To-do as the only record.
 - **A burst at 08:00.** Jobs completed overnight are all released when the window opens. The caps bound it; the first week's `skippedCap` count is read.
 - **Existing accounts.** Any account already running the old review request keeps today's behaviour until its flag is set (D5), so the worst case of this deploy is no change. UNVERIFIED: whether any account has the recipe ON today (no database read was made); the orchestrator reads `automations where recipe_key = 'review_request' and enabled` before PR 4 merges.
 - **A customer who wants to change their answer** cannot (first wins). They still see and can use the review link, and they can reply to the email (replies reach the owner via the reply-to). Accepted.
 - **Address-keyed 90 days** can suppress a second household member's request. Accepted: one email to one inbox per 90 days is the owner's rule.
 - **Timeline kind and `tasks` trigger** touch shared record and migration code; the probes above are the guard, and the trigger body is read from 0097 and not recalled.

## 7. Out of scope

Texting the check-in (a seam: the page and token are channel-free, the text composer would carry the same link once an account is registered); AI-written or auto-replies to an answer; the Google Business Profile API, reply-to-review and review import (S-34, F-170, gated); incentives or follow-up nagging; changing the referral ladder's timing; a weekly-report line; an owner-editable review link.
