What changes in the contract

  • Surfaces become translucent. The four-step ladder stays, but steps 1–3 are glass over a lit ground instead of flat fills; the "no gray blur shadows" rule becomes "no gray blur shadows, colored ambient light instead".
  • A second accent, cyan, reserved for one gradient moment per screen (hero number, sentence emphasis, the active rail) and for a second data series.
  • One faint grid and two radial glows on the page ground; everything else unchanged, including the fonts.

Trade-offs

  • The least disruptive: every existing component keeps its shape and copy. The gain is atmosphere and hierarchy, not novelty.
  • Backdrop blur costs a little GPU on long tables; needs a solid fallback for the drawer over a scrolling list.
  • Light theme is the harder twin: the aurora must drop to a whisper or it reads as smudged.
  • White-label is easy: a client's brand color replaces violet; cyan is derived from it.

Best for

  • An operator tool you use every day: calm at 7 AM, impressive in a demo, and still recognisably BIS.
  • Teams who want depth without a louder palette.
